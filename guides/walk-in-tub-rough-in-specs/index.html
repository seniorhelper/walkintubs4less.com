Walk-In Tub Rough-In and Spec Sheet Decoder

A walk-in tub spec sheet tells your installer six things before demo day: drain location, door swing, circuit, hot water, floor load and access panel placement. On one 30 x 60 model, the drain sits 9.5 inches from the side wall and 15.25 inches from the back wall.

In short

  • Drain location is measured from the side wall and the back wall. Match it to your existing drain or plan to move it.
  • Jetted and air tubs need GFCI-protected power, often a dedicated 20-amp circuit. Soakers need none.
  • Water is heavy: 75 gallons weighs about 620 pounds before you add the tub and the bather.
  • The tub must sit dead level on all of its feet, or the door seal and drain will not work as designed.
  • Plan a removable access panel. Several makers require service access to keep the warranty.

What is on a walk-in tub spec sheet?

Most spec sheets are one or two pages with a drawing, a dimension table and an electrical chart. The numbers look similar from brand to brand, but the labels are not always the same, so here is how to read the common ones. We used two published sheets that point buyers to SWCORP.com for warranty registration (a 28 x 53 and a 30 x 60) as examples, plus the Access Tubs and Ella's Bubbles installation documents.

The 28 x 53 sheet lists a tolerance of plus or minus half an inch, and both sheets tell you to measure on site before building the surround. Treat the sheet as the starting point and the tub on the floor as the final word.

Where is the drain on a walk-in tub?

The drain is at one end, under the seat or under the foot area depending on the model, and it is listed as left or right drain. You read it standing outside the tub, facing the back wall. If your existing drain is on the right and you order a left-drain tub, your plumber will be opening the floor.

The Access Tubs manual for its 3060WCA calls for a 1½-inch sanitary drain line in UPC-approved PVC, and says to prepare the subfloor at the drain and make sure the area is level. Ella's Bubbles uses gravity drains with what it calls dual drain technology and says an average-size Ella tub drains in about 80 seconds under ideal conditions. Drain speed depends heavily on your home's drain line, so take any drain time as a best case.

What are the electrical requirements for a walk-in tub?

Every maker we read requires GFCI protection for any tub with a motor, blower or heater, installed by a licensed electrician. The circuit size depends on the therapy package, and this is where buyers get surprised, because a combination tub can need more than a basic bathroom circuit.

How much does a filled walk-in tub weigh?

More than most people expect. A gallon of water weighs about 8.3 pounds, so the water alone in a deep walk-in tub can outweigh the tub several times over. The Access Tubs manual puts it simply: the subfloor must be able to support the total weight of the bathtub, the bather and the water.

Most ground-floor bathrooms on a concrete slab are not a concern. Second-floor bathrooms, older joists, past water damage or a tub that will sit across joist spans are worth a look from a contractor before you order. We do not give structural sign-off by phone; a local contractor or engineer should check the floor in person.

Why does leveling matter so much?

A walk-in tub door seals by pressure against a gasket, and the geometry only works when the tub is level. Both SWCORP-registered sheets say the tub must be "completely level with all leveling feet touching the floor" for the door, seal and drain to function. The Access Tubs manual is blunter: failure to level the tub correctly will result in misalignment, and the door may leak. If your floor slopes, plan for shimming or floor prep in the installer's quote.

Where does the access panel go?

Pumps, blowers, heaters and drain parts live under the tub skirt and behind removable front panels. The Access Tubs manual says providing service access per code is the installer's or owner's responsibility, and its warranty checklist asks that the unit be installed with access to both plumbing and electrical connections. Ella's Bubbles recommends wall access panels for future servicing. The SWCORP sheets add that jet, pump and blower locations are fixed at the factory and cannot be moved, so plan the surround around them, not the other way around.

How big a water heater does a walk-in tub need?

Bigger than most standard tubs. Ella's Bubbles recommends the largest water heater your home can accommodate and "highly recommend[s]" 75 gallons or more for average-to-large tubs. The fill figures on the two sheets above, 60 to 80 gallons with a bather inside, explain why. Inlet water temperature, heater type and recovery rate all change the answer. Aging Safely Baths has a detailed guide on how to size a water heater for a walk-in tub with worked examples, and our specs page lists the capacity for each model we carry.

A pre-install checklist for you or your contractor

  1. Confirm overall dimensions against the alcove, and order an extension kit if the opening is longer.
  2. Match drain side and drain offsets to the existing drain, or price the move.
  3. Confirm door swing and the clear floor space the door needs.
  4. Run the right GFCI circuit for the package; dedicated where the maker says so.
  5. Check water heater size and recovery against the tub's fill volume.
  6. Have the floor checked for the full filled weight, especially upstairs.
  7. Level the tub on every foot before the surround goes on.
  8. Fill and test for leaks before closing anything in. Access Tubs says to fill above the highest jet, wait 30 minutes, then run the pumps for 10 minutes.
  9. Leave access panels removable, and register the warranty.

Walk-in tub rough-in FAQ

What are the rough-in dimensions for a walk-in tub?

They vary by model. As an example, one 30 x 60 walk-in tub lists its drain 9.5 inches from the side wall and 15.25 inches from the back wall, and a 28 x 53 model lists 11.5 and 14 inches. Always use the spec sheet for your exact model and measure on site.

Does a walk-in tub need a dedicated circuit?

Powered models often do. Access Tubs requires one dedicated 20-amp GFCI circuit for its air model, and Ella's Bubbles requires a dedicated GFCI-protected line. Some spec sheets list a 15-amp GFCI circuit for basic air or whirlpool packages. Soaking tubs need no power.

What size drain does a walk-in tub need?

The Access Tubs 3060WCA manual calls for a 1½-inch sanitary drain line in UPC-approved PVC. Check your model's installation manual, since requirements can differ by brand.

Can a walk-in tub go on a second floor?

Often, yes, but the floor has to carry the tub, the water and the bather. Water alone weighs about 8.3 pounds per gallon, so a 75-gallon fill is roughly 620 pounds. Have a local contractor or engineer check the framing first.

Why does my walk-in tub need an access panel?

Pumps, blowers, heaters and drain parts sit under the tub. Makers require service access, and some list it on the warranty checklist, so a removable panel keeps repairs simple and protects your coverage.
