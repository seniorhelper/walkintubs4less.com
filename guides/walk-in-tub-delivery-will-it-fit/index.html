Walk-In Tub Delivery: Will It Fit Through My Door?

Most walk-in tubs will fit through a typical interior door, but not all of them, and the bathroom door is rarely the only tight spot. Of the 61 walk-in tubs with published dimensions in our catalog, 37 are 30 inches wide or narrower and 7 are 33 inches or wider. Measure before you order.

In short

  • Walk-in tubs arrive as one solid piece. They do not come apart to fit a narrow door.
  • Measure the clear opening of every door on the route with the door open, plus hall widths and every turn.
  • The tub's width is usually the number that has to clear the door. Its length is what has to make the turns.
  • Pick a delivery level that matches your route: curbside, threshold, or white glove to the bathroom.
  • If the route is tight, tell us before you order. A narrower model or a different path usually solves it.

Do walk-in tubs come in pieces?

No. Ella's Bubbles states that its walk-in tubs "are delivered as one unit," and Retirement Living's sizing guide says walk-in tubs are almost always delivered as one solid unit. The frame, door, seat and plumbing are built and tested together at the factory. That is good for the door seal and bad for a 28-inch hallway, which is why the measuring happens before you buy, not when the tub is in the driveway.

How wide are walk-in tubs?

Walk-in tubs run from about 26 to 36 inches wide in the lines we carry, with 30 and 32 inches being the most common. We counted the published widths for every model in our catalog that has a full spec listing.

How do I measure my home for walk-in tub delivery?

Ella's Bubbles tells buyers to measure the width of the doorways from the main entrance all the way to the bathroom, including all turning angles. That is the right instinct. Here is the step-by-step we would give a family member.

  1. Pick the route. Front door, garage door or patio door: whichever has the fewest turns and no stairs is usually the best path, even if it is longer.
  2. Measure each door's clear opening. Open the door fully and measure from the face of the open door (or the stop on the hinge side) to the stop on the latch side. That number is smaller than the door slab, and it is the one that matters.
  3. Measure hall widths at the narrowest point, including handrails, radiators, baseboard heaters and furniture you cannot move.
  4. Measure every turn. Note the hall width on both sides of the corner. A long tub turning from one narrow hall into another narrow door is the most common snag.
  5. Measure the bathroom door last, and the space inside it. Some bathrooms have a vanity or toilet close to the door that blocks the swing of a long tub.
  6. Note stairs, including a single step at the entry, and any landing where the tub would have to turn.
  7. Write it down and compare with the tub's outer width, height and length from its spec sheet.

Tubs are usually carried upright, so the width has to clear each door. Length is what has to swing through the turns. If a doorway is close, an installer can often remove the door from its hinges, and sometimes the stop molding, to gain a little room. That is a job for the crew, not something to plan on without asking.

How should a walk-in tub be carried?

Manufacturers are specific about this, and following it protects both the tub and the warranty. The Access Tubs owner's manual says to remove all four access panels before carrying the tub so they do not fall out and break, never to carry it by the panels, never to lift it by the plumbing, and to lift and carry it only by the frame. It also says to inspect the plumbing for fittings that may have loosened in transit. Protect floors and door frames with blankets or cardboard, and take the tub through doors slowly with one person guiding the front.

What if the tub will not fit through my door?

There are usually more options than people think. In rough order of cost:

  • Choose a narrower model. Thirteen models in our catalog are 28.25 inches wide or less, and some keep a comfortable seat in a smaller shell. The compare tool shows widths side by side.
  • Use a different route. A patio or garage door is often wider than the front door, with fewer turns.
  • Remove the door and stops on the tightest opening, if your installer agrees it is worthwhile.
  • Consider a shorter tub if the problem is a turn, not a door. Shorter tubs swing through corners more easily.
  • Widen a doorway as part of the remodel. This is a bigger job, but it can also help with walkers and wheelchairs later.

Walk-in tub delivery and fit FAQ

Will a walk-in tub fit through a standard door?

Many will, because most walk-in tubs are 26 to 32 inches wide. Of 61 models with published widths in our catalog, 37 are 30 inches wide or narrower. Measure the clear opening of each door with the door fully open, and compare it with the tub's outer width.

Do walk-in tubs come apart for delivery?

No. Walk-in tubs are delivered as one solid unit, with the frame, door and seat built together at the factory. Removable access panels come off for carrying, but the tub itself does not break down.

What is the narrowest walk-in tub?

In our catalog the narrowest models are 26 inches wide, such as the Hydro Dimensions 2645 at 45 x 26 inches. Six models share that width.

Does curbside delivery bring the tub inside?

No. Curbside delivery leaves the tub at the end of the driveway, usually off a lift gate truck. You or your contractor carry it in. Room-of-choice and white glove delivery bring it to the bathroom.

How many people does it take to carry a walk-in tub?

Plan on at least two strong adults. Published weights we read run from about 120 to 178 pounds before packaging, and the tub should be carried only by its frame, with the access panels removed first.
