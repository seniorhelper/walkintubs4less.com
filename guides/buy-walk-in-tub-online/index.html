How to Buy a Walk-In Tub Online Without Getting Burned

You can buy a walk-in tub online and have it shipped to your door, but the order is the easy part. Most walk-in tubs travel as one solid piece on a freight truck, and some manufacturer spec sheets give you only 5 business days after delivery to report damage or missing parts. This checklist walks you through the rest.

In short

  • Get the model's spec sheet before you pay: door side, drain side, electrical and water heater needs are fixed at the factory.
  • Know your delivery level. Curbside means the driver leaves it at the end of the driveway.
  • Inspect the crate and the tub before you sign, take photos, and keep every piece of packaging until the tub works.
  • Read the return terms. One manufacturer manual we read charges a 20% restocking fee plus all original freight.
  • Line up a licensed installer first. Several warranties are void without one.

Can you really buy a walk-in tub online?

Yes. Big-box sites, marketplace sellers and authorized dealers all ship walk-in tubs to homes across the country. We ship supply-only tubs to all 50 states and Canada, so the buyer and the bathroom are often in different places, like an adult child ordering for a parent a few states away. What changes online is who carries the risk. In a showroom sale, the seller owns the delivery, the measurement and the fit. Online, you own most of that unless you pay for a package that includes it.

That is not a reason to avoid buying online. It is a reason to buy with a plan. The buyers who have a bad experience usually skip one of three steps: they order the wrong door or drain side, they sign for a damaged crate, or they find out after delivery that nobody local will install it.

What does "supply only" actually include?

Supply only means you are buying the tub and its factory parts, shipped to an address you choose. It does not include carrying it into the bathroom, removing the old tub, plumbing, electrical, wall surrounds or haul-away. Our supply-only option adds free technical support for you or your contractor, which matters more than people expect the first time a plumber sees a pressure-sealed door.

What ships in the box varies by model. A Costco.ca listing for an American Standard 30 x 60 walk-in tub, for example, lists a Roman tub faucet with hand shower, a 13-jet whirlpool, an in-line heater, a quick-drain pump, a support frame with 5 adjustable leveling feet and an extension kit. Other tubs ship without a faucet or without wall extensions. Ask for the packing list in writing.

What should you check before you click buy?

Walk-in tubs are not like a standard bathtub where any 60-inch alcove takes any 60-inch tub. These are the specs that decide whether the tub you order is the tub you can use.

  1. Door side and drain side. A left-drain tub and a right-drain tub are different products. Stand outside the alcove facing the back wall and note which end the existing drain is on.
  2. Door swing. Inward-swinging doors need room inside the tub for the bather to move around the door. Outward doors need clear floor space outside the tub. We break this down on the walk-in tub specs page.
  3. Electrical. Jetted and air models need GFCI-protected power. One spec sheet we read calls for a 15-amp GFCI circuit for an air or whirlpool tub and a 20-amp GFCI circuit for a whirlpool with an in-line heater. A soaker needs none.
  4. Hot water. Ella's Bubbles recommends a water heater of 75 gallons or more for average-to-large tubs. Spec sheets we read list average fills of 60 to 80 gallons with a bather inside.
  5. Delivery path. Measure every door, hall and turn from the truck to the bathroom. See our guide on whether a walk-in tub will fit through your door before you order.
  6. Installer and warranty rules. One SWCORP-registered spec sheet says installation by a certified installer is required or the warranty is void, and that registration is required to activate it.

How does walk-in tub freight delivery work?

Walk-in tubs ship by LTL freight, usually strapped to a pallet or in a crate. Ella's Bubbles says plainly that its tubs are delivered as one unit, and most brands work the same way. The tubs are not light: published weights we found run from 120 pounds for a 28 x 53 soaker to 178 pounds for a 30 x 60 jetted model, before the crate.

The service level on your order decides where the driver stops:

  • Curbside: the tub comes off the truck on a lift gate and stays at the end of the driveway. Costco.ca's listing says curbside "does not include setup, assembly, or packaging removal." A Lowe's walk-in tub listing we checked says "Curbside delivery only."
  • Threshold or garage: the driver brings it to the first door or into the garage. Less tipping and dragging than curbside.
  • Room of choice or white glove: a crew carries it to the bathroom, and some packages include unpacking, old-tub removal and installation. Our white glove package covers delivery, removal, install and haul-away in one price.

Lead times vary by brand and stock. Costco.ca, for one, quotes an estimated 20 to 30 business days. We confirm lead time by phone for each model because factory schedules change.

What should you do when the freight truck arrives?

This is the step that costs people the most money, because it happens fast and the driver is usually in a hurry. Plan to be home, have a second person there, and keep your phone charged for photos.

  1. Walk around the crate before you sign. Look for crushed corners, punctures, forklift marks and wet spots. The Access Tubs owner's manual says to photograph any damage and notify the carrier.
  2. Write specific notes on the delivery receipt. "Crate crushed at right rear corner" protects you. A signature with no notes tells the carrier everything arrived fine.
  3. Open it as soon as you can, even if the crate looks perfect. The same Access Tubs manual warns that surface damage not reported at inspection "will be assumed to be installation related."
  4. Check the plumbing and fittings. Manufacturers warn that fittings can loosen in transit, and that you should never lift the tub by its plumbing or access panels. Carry it by the frame.
  5. Report within the deadline in writing. The SWCORP sheets give 5 business days for damage or missing parts. Email beats a phone call because it leaves a time stamp.
  6. Keep all packaging. Access Tubs says returns are only accepted in the original packaging, and not to throw shipping material away until installation is complete.

Who installs a walk-in tub you buy online?

Plan this before the tub ships. A walk-in tub install usually touches three trades: demolition and framing, plumbing, and electrical for jetted or heated models. Ella's Bubbles tells buyers to hire licensed, experienced contractors who know local codes, and the Access Tubs manual says every walk-in tub must be installed by a licensed plumber, electrician or contractor or the warranty is void.

If you already have a plumber you trust, supply only is often the better value. Send your installer the spec sheet the day you order so they can confirm the drain location, the circuit and the access panel. If you do not have someone lined up, the installed route removes the guesswork. For the full picture of what a bring-your-own-installer purchase includes, Aging Safely Baths explains how supply-only orders work with your own contractor.

Buying a walk-in tub online: FAQ

Is it cheaper to buy a walk-in tub online?

The tub itself is often less expensive when you buy supply only, because you are not paying for installation in the same price. Your total cost still includes freight, removal of the old tub, plumbing and any electrical work, so compare the full project, not the tub alone.

What does curbside delivery mean for a walk-in tub?

Curbside means the carrier lowers the tub off the truck on a lift gate and leaves it at the end of your driveway. Carrying it inside, unpacking it and removing the packaging are not included.

What should I do if my walk-in tub arrives damaged?

Photograph the crate and the damage, write specific notes on the delivery receipt before you sign, and report it to the seller in writing right away. Some makers allow only 5 business days to report damage or missing parts.

Can I return a walk-in tub I bought online?

It depends on the seller. Terms we read range from a 90-day retail return window to a 30-day request window with a 20% restocking fee plus original freight. Most require the original packaging, so keep it until the tub is installed and working.

Do I need an electrician for a walk-in tub?

For jetted, air or heated models, yes. Spec sheets we read call for GFCI-protected 15-amp or 20-amp circuits depending on the package, installed by a licensed electrician. A plain soaking tub has no electrical needs.
