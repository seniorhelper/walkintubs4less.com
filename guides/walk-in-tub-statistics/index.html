Walk-In Tub Statistics 2026: Real Numbers, Every One Sourced

The US had 64.6 million people aged 65 and older on July 1, 2025, up 16.2% since 2020, and 75% of adults 50 and older say they want to stay in their current home as they age. Every number below has a source, plus product counts from the 71 tubs we sell.

In short

  • Older adults are the fastest-growing age group, and the Census Bureau projects they will outnumber children by 2034.
  • In the 2019 American Housing Survey, 5.4% of households with someone 65 or older reported difficulty using the bathroom, rising to 13.4% with someone 85 or older.
  • In 2008, 68.3% of nonfatal bathroom injuries treated in US emergency rooms happened in or around the tub or shower.
  • Across 71 walk-in tubs in our catalog, 42 have inward-swinging doors and 29 swing outward.
  • "80% of falls happen in the bathroom" has no primary source. We do not use it, and neither should you.

How fast is the older population growing?

Quickly, and the growth is spread across the country. The US Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates count 64,617,088 people aged 65 and older on July 1, 2025, an increase of 8,988,997 (16.2%) since April 2020. The South grew fastest at 17.5%, followed by the West at 16.5%. Looking ahead, the Census Bureau projects that by 2030 one in every five Americans will be of retirement age, and that by 2034 older adults will outnumber children for the first time in US history.

For a store like ours, the meaning is practical. More families are shopping for a parent's bathroom, often from out of state, and they need straight answers on sizes, delivery and installation rather than a sales visit.

Do older adults want to stay in their homes?

Most do. AARP's 2024 Home and Community Preferences Survey of 3,090 adults found that 75% of adults 50 and older want to stay in their current home as they age, and 73% want to stay in their community. You will still see older figures like "90% want to age in place" quoted online. That wording comes from earlier surveys with different questions, so we use the 2024 number.

How many homes are ready for aging in place?

Fewer than you might hope, depending on how you define "ready." The Census Bureau has published two reports on this, and their definitions differ, which is why the headline numbers look so different.

What do the numbers say about bathroom injuries?

The most-quoted study is still a CDC report in MMWR from 2011, using 2008 emergency room data. It estimated 234,094 nonfatal bathroom injuries among people 15 and older that year, a rate of 96.4 per 100,000. Falls caused 81.1% of those injuries, and 68.3% happened in or around the tub or shower. The injury rate climbed steeply with age, from 58.8 per 100,000 at ages 15 to 24 to 515.3 per 100,000 at 85 and older.

Note what that study does and does not say. It says 81.1% of bathroom injuries were caused by falls. It does not say 80% of falls happen in the bathroom, a claim that circulates widely with no primary source behind it. On falls in general, the CDC reports that more than one in four older adults falls each year, and the medical cost of nonfatal older-adult falls was about $80 billion in 2020. For the full fall picture, including deaths, hospital costs and which myths to ignore, see the sourced bathroom fall statistics from Aging Safely Baths.

What are the numbers on hot water and scalds?

Walk-in tubs hold a lot of hot water, and older adults are among the people most at risk of scalds. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission urges setting water heaters to 120°F and publishes how fast hot water causes a third-degree burn in most adults.

Which walk-in tub statistics should you ignore?

  • "80% of falls happen in the bathroom." No primary source. The real CDC figure is that 81.1% of bathroom injuries were caused by falls.
  • "10,000 Americans turn 65 every day." A widely repeated older estimate. Use current Census counts instead.
  • "Walk-in tubs fill in 6 to 8 minutes." Fill time depends on the tub's capacity, your faucet flow and water pressure. Use the maker's figure for your model, if it gives one.
  • "Medicare pays for walk-in tubs with a prescription." We found no CMS source for this. Original Medicare generally does not cover them.

Figures are as published by the sources below on the dates shown. No bathing product eliminates the risk of falls; products are designed to reduce risk when installed and used properly.

Walk-in tub statistics FAQ

How many Americans are 65 or older?

The US Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates count 64,617,088 people aged 65 and older on July 1, 2025, up 16.2% since April 2020.

What percentage of bathroom injuries happen in the tub or shower?

In a CDC study of 2008 emergency room data, 68.3% of nonfatal bathroom injuries among people 15 and older happened in or around the tub or shower, and 81.1% of all bathroom injuries were caused by falls.

Is it true that 80% of falls happen in the bathroom?

We could not find any primary source for that claim. It appears to be a misreading of the CDC finding that 81.1% of bathroom injuries were caused by falls, which is a different statement.

How many older adults have trouble using the bathroom at home?

In the 2019 American Housing Survey, 5.4% of households with an adult 65 or older, about 2.0 million households, reported difficulty using the bathroom. Among households with an adult 85 or older, it was 13.4%.

What is the most common walk-in tub size?

In our catalog of 61 models with published dimensions, widths of 30 and 32 inches are the most common, and the most common lengths are 52 inches and 60 inches. Most models are shorter than a standard 60-inch bathtub.
