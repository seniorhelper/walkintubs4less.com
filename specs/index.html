Rough-in: what the installer needs first

Ask for the factory drawing of the exact model and door hand before any demolition. It shows the drain location, the supply connections, the frame footprint and how far the access panel sits from the wall. Order the drain side that matches the existing drain: standing in front of the tub, is the drain on your left or your right? Moving a drain is possible but it is the single most common reason an install runs long. Our rough-in and spec sheet guide walks through every line on a spec sheet.

Electrical: soakers need nothing, jets need a circuit

A soaking walk-in has no pump and needs no power. Water jets, air jets, inline heaters and heated seats do. The National Electrical Code treats these as hydromassage bathtubs (Article 680, Part VII), which calls for ground-fault (GFCI) protection, and makers specify a dedicated circuit with the amperage on the spec sheet. A licensed electrician should read that sheet before the tub ships.

Door clearance

Inward doors swing into the tub and need no floor space in front. Outward doors swing into the room: plan clear floor at least as wide as the door so it opens fully, and keep the toilet and vanity out of its path. Lift-up doors, such as the Rane RR7 Atlantic, need overhead clearance instead.

Step-in height

The threshold is the wall you step over to get in. Lower is easier on hips and knees. The lowest published thresholds on tubs we sell: Hydro Dimensions 2851 (5 in.), Hydro Dimensions 3555 Plus (5 in.), Hydro Dimensions 3060 (5.25 in.), Hydro Dimensions 3055 (6 in.), Hydro Dimensions 3060L (6 in.), Hydro Dimensions 3155D (6 in.).

Delivery sizes and the path in

The tub travels crated, so the crate is a little larger than the shell. Measure every point on the route: the front door, hall turns, stair landings and the bathroom door, at the tightest spot including trim. As a first check, look for about 2 inches more than the tub's width. Tubs can sometimes be tipped on edge to pass a narrow door; your installer judges that on site. Our fit planner runs these numbers for you.

Hot water

You fill a walk-in after you sit down and close the door, so the heater has to deliver most of the fill in one go. Compare the gallons in the table with your water heater. Aging Safely Baths explains how to size a water heater for a walk-in tub in detail.

Electrical reference: NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, Article 680 Part VII (hydromassage bathtubs). Local codes and inspectors have the final word.
